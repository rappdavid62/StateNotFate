## 1. Sources And Disposition

### Official / primary
- CDC Suicide Data and Statistics - `https://www.cdc.gov/suicide/data/index.html`
  - Disposition: anchor source; supports public-health framing, 2024 U.S. mortality context, and demographic trends.
  - Why it matters: keeps the appendix grounded in current population-level reality.

- HHS 2024 National Strategy for Suicide Prevention - `https://www.hhs.gov/programs/prevention-and-wellness/mental-health-substance-use-disorder/national-strategy-suicide-prevention/index.html`
  - Disposition: anchor strategy; supports whole-of-society prevention language.
  - Why it matters: frames prevention as systems work, not just crisis response.

- SAMHSA 988 - `https://www.samhsa.gov/mental-health/988`
  - Disposition: crisis-routing source; public-facing urgent support context.
  - Why it matters: required U.S. crisis context for the appendix.

- WHO LIVE LIFE - `https://www.who.int/initiatives/live-life-initiative-for-suicide-prevention`
  - Disposition: global prevention model; upstream and multisector framing.
  - Why it matters: supports means safety, responsible messaging, life skills, and early support.

- NIMH Suicide Prevention - `https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/suicide-prevention`
  - Disposition: public education anchor; plain-language definitions and current research framing.
  - Why it matters: supports a serious but understandable public appendix.

### Practice frameworks
- Zero Suicide - `https://zerosuicide.edc.org/about/framework`
  - Disposition: implementation framework.
  - Why it matters: useful for systems design, workflow, and continuous improvement.

- SPRC Stanley-Brown Safety Plan - `https://sprc.org/resources/stanley-brown-safety-plan/`
  - Disposition: practical support tool.
  - Why it matters: gives the appendix a concrete safety-planning lane.

- SPRC CALM - `https://sprc.org/resources/calm-counseling-on-access-to-lethal-means/`
  - Disposition: means-safety toolkit.
  - Why it matters: supports collaborative harm-reduction language without procedural detail.

- Action Alliance postvention guideline - `https://theactionalliance.org/resource/responding-grief-trauma-and-distress-after-suicide-us-national-guidelines`
  - Disposition: postvention guidance.
  - Why it matters: lets the appendix include what happens after loss or crisis, not only before it.

### Research anchors
- Stanley et al. SPI+ - `https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29998307/`
  - Disposition: strongest practical evidence for safety planning plus follow-up.
  - Why it matters: supports the aftercare and transition sections.

- Caring contacts literature - `https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11775719/`
  - Disposition: follow-up / connection model.
  - Why it matters: supports low-friction aftercare and repeated human contact.

- Umbrella reviews on risk and social determinants - `https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(23)00207-4` and `https://doi.org/10.1093/epirev/mxaf004`
  - Disposition: research synthesis.
  - Why it matters: supports the idea that risk is stacked, not singular.

## 2. Twenty Most Pertinent Ideas For SNF

1. Suicide prevention is a public-health, clinical, community, and equity problem.
   - Supports: expands the project beyond hotline language.

2. Depression is a state, not an identity.
   - Supports: core State Not Fate framing.

3. Risk is stacked, not singular.
   - Supports: replaces one-cause narratives with realistic systems thinking.

4. Warning signs are not the same as background risk factors.
   - Supports: improves interpretation and routing.

5. Care transitions are high-risk windows.
   - Supports: strengthens same-day through 4-week follow-up language.

6. Safety planning plus follow-up is more actionable than vague reassurance.
   - Supports: concrete next-step design.

7. Community trust is prevention infrastructure.
   - Supports: makes the appendix more realistic for help-seeking behavior.

8. Means safety should be framed as collaborative harm reduction.
   - Supports: safer public language.

9. Postvention belongs in the appendix.
   - Supports: extends prevention beyond the initial crisis.

10. Perinatal and postpartum risk needs explicit treatment.
    - Supports: avoids generic coverage that misses a major high-risk lane.

11. Youth and young adults need distinct routing and support language.
    - Supports: age-aware prevention.

12. Veterans need transition-aware, trust-aware support pathways.
    - Supports: population-specific relevance.

13. LGBTQ+ prevention should center affirmation and disclosure safety.
    - Supports: equity and trust.

14. AI/AN prevention should respect sovereignty and local cultural context.
    - Supports: culturally inclusive prevention.

15. Rural and older-adult prevention must account for isolation and transport.
    - Supports: practical access reality.

16. Measurement should track engagement and routing, not fake risk scores.
    - Supports: aligns with product honesty.

17. Safe messaging matters because public language can reduce or amplify harm.
    - Supports: responsible appendix tone.

18. Fast-acting treatments belong in the educational layer, but not as promises.
    - Supports: keeps clinical claims bounded.

19. The appendix should help users decide the next human contact.
    - Supports: navigation under stress.

20. The page should feel like a serious appendix, not a self-diagnosis tool.
    - Supports: keeps the SNF identity intact.

### Recommended content patterns
- Use short role-based entries: I am in danger now, I am worried about myself, I am helping someone, I want to understand prevention, I need sources.
- Keep every non-user-facing claim traceable to an official, research, or clearly labeled nonprofit source.
- Keep clinical, lived-experience, and public-health sources visibly separated.
- Preserve the crisis context: 988 in the U.S., 911 for immediate danger.
- Do not add graphic detail or romantic framing.
