Evidence / Sources

State Not Fate uses a layered evidence model so public language stays conservative, sources stay visible, and open questions do not get presented as settled fact.

Evidence

Supported by official guidance, validated measures, or peer-reviewed findings with direct relevance to the claim being made.

Inference

A practical conclusion drawn from evidence, design limits, and observed user needs. Useful, but still a step away from direct proof.

Hypothesis

A promising idea that should be tested before it becomes project messaging, product logic, or a public promise.

Opinion

A judgment, preference, or interpretation. It may be thoughtful, but it is not evidence by itself.

Personal testimony

Meaningful lived experience that can shape design and language, but should not be generalized as universal proof.

Public claim

Anything said to outside users must be supportable, careful, and honest about the limits of what State Not Fate can do.

How To Read These Sources

The most important source question is not just whether something sounds helpful. It is what kind of source it is, what it can legitimately support, and what disposition it should have inside the project.

Official anchor

Use as baseline truth

Government or major intergovernmental pages that define crisis routing, surveillance, prevention frameworks, or clinical/public-health standards.

Implementation tool

Use to shape workflow

Toolkits and frameworks help State Not Fate explain care transitions, support planning, outreach, and safer implementation patterns.

Research anchor

Use to support a narrow claim

Peer-reviewed studies can support specific interventions or concepts, but should not be stretched into a general product promise.

Project synthesis

Use as design translation

Internal summaries, handoffs, and frameworks can consolidate sources into usable architecture, but they remain synthesis rather than primary evidence.

Loose end / watchlist

Hold until verified

Recent guideline summaries, press releases, and AI-generated notes can open a new line of inquiry, but they should stay out of public claims until checked against primary sources.

Boundary rule

Do not overclaim

State Not Fate can educate, scaffold, and route. It should not claim to diagnose, predict suicide, or replace emergency or clinical care.

Current Project Rules

Safety rule

Avoid graphic detail

No method instructions, no sensational framing, and no romanticizing suicide.

Product rule

Separate support from treatment

State Not Fate can support reflection, education, and help-seeking, but it is not a crisis service or a treatment claim.

Evidence rule

Show disposition, not just links

Every source should eventually be tagged as anchor, implementation tool, research support, synthesis, or watchlist.

Measurement rule

Track learning, not fake certainty

The project should measure engagement, scripts saved, and support routing, not assign suicide-risk scores.
