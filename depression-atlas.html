Depression Cause-and-Effect Atlas

Depression is not a character verdict. It is a systems failure that can retune energy, reward prediction, threat detection, sleep, follow-through, and the felt cost of action. Adverse childhood experiences sit upstream of that failure. They raise population risk for depression and suicidal behavior. They do not assign a personal fate, and this page never turns an ACE count into a personal risk score.

What this page will not do

No ACE quiz. No suicide probability. No public LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH. No “safe” or “unsafe” clearance. Odds ratios below are population associations from research samples. A person with many ACEs may never become depressed. A person with none may still be in a severe episode. Hard safety rules beat any background statistic. This is education inside an adjunctive tool, not diagnosis, treatment, or a replacement for professional care.

The dose pattern, read correctly

ACEs are categories of childhood abuse, neglect, and household dysfunction, usually counted before age 18. The original CDC–Kaiser work and later reviews show a graded pattern: as the count rises, rates of adult depression and suicide attempts rise with it. The pattern is strong. It is not linear destiny, and retrospective studies usually show larger odds than prospective ones.

Dube et al., 2001, JAMA, is the suicide-attempt paper. Each step up the ACE score raised attempt odds by roughly 50–70% in that cohort. Population attributable fractions there were large (about 67% of lifetime attempts associated with ≥1 ACE) because ACEs were common — attributable fraction is not individual probability.
≥4 vs none, pooled adult studies Mental-ill-health odds in the “more than 3 to 6” band; about fourfold across distress and disorder indicators Self-directed violence in the strongest band (odds ratios greater than 7), with high heterogeneity Evidence Hughes et al., Lancet Public Health, 2017. 37 studies, 253,719 adults, general samples, ≥4 vs 0. Wide uncertainty on the violence estimates. Do not treat the top of that band as a personal multiplier.
Prospective studies OR 1.61 (1.45–1.76) Suicidality OR 1.28 (1.13–1.43). PTSD OR 2.26. Anxiety OR 1.78. Evidence Murray et al., 2025 systematic review. Smaller than retrospective odds. Maltreatment and violence ACEs were stronger depression predictors than household ACEs. Recall bias is real. The signal does not disappear when recall bias is reduced.
Already in an affective disorder The diagnosis is already present Suicidal behavior OR 1.98 (1.74–2.26). Sexual abuse OR 2.24 (1.90–2.64). Ideation OR 2.16. Attempts OR 1.95. Evidence 2025 European Psychiatry meta-analysis, 41 studies, 19,588 people. ACEs are not fully “used up” by the depression or bipolar diagnosis. They still mark higher suicidal behavior inside the clinical group.
Type, not just count Youth-onset depression OR 2.14 (1.93–2.37) after early-life adversity. Emotional abuse OR 4.25 (3.04–5.94). Threat OR 2.60 vs deprivation OR 1.76. Adult suicide attempt: sexual abuse OR 3.17 (2.76–3.64), physical abuse OR 2.52, emotional abuse OR 2.49, complex abuse OR 5.18 (2.52–10.63). Evidence Youth: 2024 meta-analysis, 87 studies, 213,006. Adults: Angelakis et al., 2019, 68 studies, n = 261,660. Emotional abuse is not a minor category. Threat loads depression earlier and harder than deprivation, on average.

Dube and colleagues also showed partial mediation. Depressed affect, self-reported alcoholism, and illicit drug use carried part of the ACE-to-attempt relationship. That matters mechanically. The childhood load often arrives in adult life through the same substrates this project already runs: mood state, substances, and a nervous system that has stopped expecting effort to pay.

How the load enters the machine

Evidence → synthesis

Threat

Physical abuse, sexual abuse, and violence in the home bias salience and danger learning. In the atlas this feeds threat bias, nocturnal vigilance, and self-attack. It is not a moral stain. It is a calibration that made sense in the original environment and now over-fires.

Evidence → synthesis

Deprivation

Emotional and physical neglect, chaos, and missing care reduce expected reward and cognitive bandwidth. This is the closest ACE path to the project’s core observation: effort starts to feel expensive, and payoff starts to feel absent. Emotional abuse and neglect are high-yield, not soft.

Synthesis

Household dysfunction

A household member with mental illness, substance problems, or incarceration, or a loss of a parent, adds unpredictability. The child learns that other people and tomorrow are unstable inputs. Adult social shrinkage and substance coping often continue that lesson.

Synthesis

Body, not biography homework

Toxic stress literature links early adversity to HPA-axis wear, inflammatory tone, sleep disruption, and reward-circuit blunting. Those are mechanisms, not a lab you can run in the browser. The usable translation is substrate first: light, sleep timing, food, movement, one safe contact, treatment if indicated. Childhood narrative work is not the Level 1 lever.

What State Not Fate does with this

Year 1 job stays de-chaos

A high ACE load is a reason to make the floor smaller, not a reason to start with identity repair, trauma processing, or a five-year ambition. Stabilization is the correct early job: wake window, light, water, food, meds if prescribed, one reset.

Proof against a trained prediction

If the childhood environment taught “effort will not be met,” the depressed state will reuse that prediction. The counter is not encouragement. It is one action small enough to finish, written down so a bad hour cannot erase it.

Treatment lane stays open

Trauma-focused therapy, medication when indicated, and substance treatment are clinical tools. The atlas can point at the lane. It does not dose, time, or replace it. Severe self-neglect, mania, psychosis, or active withdrawal needs a person and a service, not a self-map.

988 is not a failure state

Suicidal thoughts can appear in severe depression with or without ACEs. Contact 988, a trusted person, or 911 when there is immediate danger. Means safety is a conversation with a person, not a set of instructions on this page.
